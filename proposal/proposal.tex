\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries When does attribution patching agree with\\ activation patching across tasks and languages?}\\[0.5em]
Hrishiraj Mitra (2023111037)\\
CL3.412 Mechanistic Interpretability of Language Models
\end{center}

\section{Problem}
Activation patching (AP) is the standard causal test for which attention heads and MLPs a language model uses for a behaviour, but it costs one forward pass per component. Attribution patching (AttrP) replaces those passes with a single gradient, and is therefore the tool of choice at scale. AttrP is only useful where it ranks components as AP does, and that has been checked almost exclusively in English. This project asks where the approximation holds and where it breaks once the task, the language and the model change.

A running example is subject--verb agreement, scored on the next token after a prefix:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(1)] \hi{चैनल भविष्य में ऐसी घटना की पुनरावृत्ति नहीं होने का वचन देता} \;\textbf{\hi{है}} vs.\ \hi{हों}\\
  {\small `The channel promises that such an incident will not recur' \textit{(is.3SG vs.\ be.3PL)}}
  \item[(2)] Yes, lovely creature, \;\textbf{is} vs.\ am
\end{itemize}
The model should prefer the bolded form. Pairs of this kind come from MultiBLiMP; the same procedure applies to the other tasks in Section~3.

\textbf{Notation.} Let $m = \mathrm{logit}(y^{+}) - \mathrm{logit}(y^{-})$ be the logit difference between the correct and incorrect answer. Each prompt has a clean version and a corrupted version in which the cue is altered. For a component $c$ with activation $a_c$, patching copies $a_c$ from a \emph{source} run into a \emph{target} run; in the denoising direction the source is clean and the target corrupted, and in the noising direction the reverse. The AP effect and its AttrP estimate are
\[
\Delta_c^{\mathrm{AP}} = m\big(\text{target} \mid a_c \leftarrow a_c^{\mathrm{src}}\big) - m(\text{target}),
\qquad
\hat\Delta_c = \big(a_c^{\mathrm{src}} - a_c^{\mathrm{tgt}}\big)^{\!\top} \nabla_{a_c} m \,\big|_{\text{target}} .
\]
Within an experimental \emph{cell} (one model, task, language and direction), agreement between the vectors $\Delta^{\mathrm{AP}}$ and $\hat\Delta$ is measured by Spearman $\rho$ over heads, Pearson $r$ over MLP layers, and overlap of the 14 highest-$|\Delta|$ heads. A cell is classed as \emph{agreeing} when $\rho \ge 0.90$ and top-14 overlap $\ge 0.5$.

\textbf{Research questions.}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.8em,itemsep=0.1em]
  \item[(RQ1)] Does AttrP--AP agreement differ between tasks, and between English and Hindi on the same task?
  \item[(RQ2)] Does it differ between attention heads and MLPs?
  \item[(RQ3)] Does it depend on the model and on how heavily its tokenizer fragments the prompt?
  \item[(RQ4)] Where first-order AttrP fails, does integrated-gradients attribution (EAP-IG) recover agreement?
\end{description}
The unit of analysis is the component-level effect estimate. Path-level circuit discovery is not attempted, and comparing which heads matter in English versus Hindi is deferred until the reliability of the estimates is known.

\section{Motivation and related work}
AttrP was introduced as a first-order approximation to AP for large models (Nanda, 2023) and performs competitively with automated circuit discovery (Syed et al., 2024). Kram\'ar et al.\ (2024) identify settings where it fails, notably saturated attention and MLP nonlinearities, and propose corrections. Hanna et al.\ (2024) show that integrating gradients along the path from corrupted to clean input gives more faithful attributions, and the MIB benchmark ranks this variant among the strongest localisation methods (Mueller et al., 2025). Methodological guidance on metrics and corruptions for AP is given by Zhang and Nanda (2024) and Heimersheim and Nanda (2024).

All of these evaluations use English prompts. Hindi differs from English in properties that could plausibly affect a linear approximation: verb-final word order, gender agreement on verbs, and Devanagari text that many tokenizers split into several tokens per word. If AttrP rankings degrade under such changes, attribution-based findings in non-English settings would require AP confirmation; if they do not, the cheaper method can be used with more confidence. The contribution is an empirical characterisation of agreement across conditions, not a new attribution method.

Existing resources are reused. MultiBLiMP~1.0 (Jumelet et al., 2025; \url{github.com/jumelet/multiblimp}) provides 1{,}447 Hindi and 770 English agreement minimal pairs. The EAP-IG library (\url{github.com/hannamw/EAP-IG}) serves as an independent cross-check of the project's TransformerLens implementation, which has already reproduced the known GPT-2 small IOI pattern over five seeds (head $\rho \approx 0.99$, MLP $r \approx -0.93$). That result is the English reference point for this study.

\section{Method}
\textbf{Design.} Cells are formed by crossing the following factors; each cell is run with five seeds, where a seed fixes the sampled prompts.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Tasks:} subject--verb agreement (MultiBLiMP; answer pair taken at the first word where the grammatical and ungrammatical sentences differ); country-to-capital recall, as a control with little syntax; indirect object identification (IOI), with Hindi templates written so that the prompt ends immediately before the recipient name, as required by verb-final order.
  \item \emph{Languages:} English and Hindi in Devanagari. Romanised Hindi and ergative \hi{ने}-perfective agreement are optional extensions.
  \item \emph{Models}, all fitting one 11\,GB GPU: GPT-2 small (English only, reference); Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama-3.2-1B (English and Hindi). Gemma-3-1B or Sarvam-1 are added if TransformerLens supports them.
  \item \emph{Components:} attention heads and MLP layers, analysed separately.
  \item \emph{Direction:} denoising (primary) and noising (secondary).
\end{itemize}
Corrupted prompts are other items of equal token length from the same task. Hindi templates are reviewed by a native speaker before any result is reported.

\textbf{Inclusion criterion.} Agreement is only meaningful if the model performs the task. Before attribution, each (model, task, language) combination is screened on 100 prompts and retained only if clean accuracy $\ge 0.80$, clean $m \ge 0.5$, corrupted $m \le 0.25$, and at least 90\% of answers are single tokens. Combinations that fail are listed in a coverage table and make no contribution to agreement claims.

\textbf{Measurements.} For every retained cell: head $\rho$; a restricted $\rho$ over heads with $|\Delta^{\mathrm{AP}}| > 0.05$ (threshold set in advance, to avoid inflation by many near-zero heads); top-$k$ overlap for $k \in \{5, 10, 14, 20\}$; MLP $r$; and wall-clock cost of each method. Two descriptors of the cell are also recorded: \emph{effect concentration}, the share of total $|\Delta^{\mathrm{AP}}|$ carried by the top 14 heads, and \emph{tokenizer fertility}, the mean number of tokens per word in the prompts.

\textbf{Analysis.} Agreement is reported per factor level and for each factor combination. English and Hindi are compared on matched (model, task) pairs with a paired bootstrap over seeds. To address \emph{when} agreement holds, cell-level $\rho$ is related to clean $m$, effect concentration and tokenizer fertility. Node-level EAP-IG, with the number of integration steps swept on one seed, is computed on the same cells and compared with AttrP.

\textbf{Hypotheses.}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=0.05em]
  \item[(H1)] In retained cells, head-level AttrP agrees with AP ($\rho \ge 0.90$).
  \item[(H2)] MLP-level AttrP does not agree with AP ($r < 0$), as in the GPT-2 reference.
  \item[(H3)] On matched (model, task) pairs, agreement is lower in Hindi than in English.
  \item[(H4)] Agreement decreases with tokenizer fertility and increases with clean $m$.
  \item[(H5)] EAP-IG yields higher MLP agreement than first-order AttrP.
\end{description}

\textbf{Threats to validity.} The inclusion criterion may remove exactly the Hindi cells where disagreement would be largest; the coverage table makes this visible. Agreement is measured against AP, which itself depends on the corruption chosen; the corruption scheme is fixed per task in advance. Hindi data are drawn from one benchmark and from templates, so conclusions are limited to one Indic language and to controlled prompts. Uncertainty is reported as bootstrap intervals over seeds.
